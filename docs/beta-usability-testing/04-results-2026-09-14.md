## Step 1: Disabled formatter chain is absent from inspection

`:Plait inspect capabilities formatting` showed an active capability, Conform,
the `local.lang.python` dependent, and configuration values/sources for
`lsp_fallback`, mappings, on-save formatting, and timeout. It did not show the
Python formatter chain or the provenance of its explicit disable override.

The reported effective value was `lsp_fallback: if_no_formatter`. Disabling
`formatting.by_filetype.python` disables that formatter chain; it does not by
itself disable Python formatting through LSP. The procedure's expectation that
“Python formatting is deliberately disabled” therefore needs more precise wording.

Follow-up acceptance criteria:

- Inspection explains the disabled Python formatter chain and its override provenance.
- Inspection distinguishes a disabled chain from all formatting being disabled.
- The procedure states that LSP fallback remains enabled in this example.

## Step 2: Native hover mapping produces a collision warning

Inspection reported `language.mapping_collision` for normal-mode `K` in buffer 1,
with action `language.hover`. Its repair instructed the owner to remove the mapping
or configure another key, although the owner identified the existing mapping as
Neovim's native hover mapping providing the intended action.

Source review: `lua/plait/language_mappings.lua` treats an existing buffer-local
mapping whose callback is not Plait's owned callback as a collision. It has no
special handling for a compatible native mapping. This explains the warning;
the actual mapping identity was not independently reproduced.

Follow-up acceptance criteria:

- A recognized compatible native hover mapping remains intact without a repair warning.
- Unrelated mappings still receive ownership protection and actionable diagnostics.
- Preflight and attachment-time reconciliation agree on compatible native mappings.

Open decision: rely on native hover defaults, or retain the Plait mapping default
and recognize native compatibility. Removing Plait's default needs consideration
of its supported Neovim versions and capability-action behavior.

## Step 4: Failed application is too easy to miss

Switching between configurations with and without a foreign format mapping did
not make the faulty configuration immediately apparent. The owner had to notice
missing managed effects and explicitly inspect diagnostics.

Follow-up acceptance criteria:

- When preflight blocks application, concise visible feedback says application was blocked.
- Feedback names the collision, gives a repair, and points to detailed inspection.
- Routine warnings do not generate a stream of notifications.
- Existing feedback settings are considered before adding a new notification policy.

The report does not establish that preflight failed to protect the foreign mapping;
this finding concerns discoverability of the blocked application.

## General: Inspection mixes global health with current-file relevance

The owner found inspection verbose, especially its file/line/path source lists.
Formatting appeared `[DEGRADED]` with `tool.absent` and Lua/TypeScript dependents
while editing Lua, even though the absent tool concerned TypeScript.

Follow-up acceptance criteria:

- Capability inspection clearly labels whether health is configuration-wide or current-buffer scoped.
- Degradation names the affected tool and filetype rather than only `tool.absent`.
- Routine output emphasizes effective behavior, disabled contributions, and required repairs.
- Detailed provenance remains available without dominating routine inspection.

Open decision: concise default output with an explicit detail option, configurable
verbosity, or both. Buffer-specific relevance must not conceal configuration-wide
problems or make completed-snapshot inspection appear to be live buffer status.
